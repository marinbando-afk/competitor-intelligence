How one makeup brand made $50K by spotting a competitor's strategy early

A competitor quietly rebuilt their whole Q4 playbook. This foundation brand read the move 10 days early — and got there first. Here's the play, signal by signal.

Mia runs a seven-figure makeup brand. Her hero product is a shade-adapting foundation — the kind that adjusts to your skin tone in seconds, the kind TikTok loves. Like most ecom founders, she used to check on her competitors the same way everyone does: opening their Instagram at 11pm, scrolling the Meta Ad Library on her phone, screenshotting things and forgetting where she saved them.

Then one Tuesday in July, her morning brief flagged something that didn't look like routine activity.

❗ 📣 Intercepted · AdsTue, 14 Jul · 8:00 AM
11 new ads launched in 48 hours — every one pushes a "Flawless Base Kit" at 25% off, driving to a brand-new advertorial page. Dominant hook: "I threw out 6 foundations after finding this routine."

One competitor — a brand roughly her size — had gone from running the same five product ads for months to launching eleven new creatives in two days. All of them pushed a kit: foundation, primer and blending sponge, bundled. None of them pushed single products.

"On its own, one new ad means nothing. Eleven new ads all singing the same song? That's not testing. That's a strategy being rolled out."

The next two mornings told the whole story

15 Jul
Website: a new kit SKU appeared on the competitor's storefront — their three bestsellers packaged together, anchored against $78, selling at $58 with a countdown timer.
16 Jul
Email: cadence doubled. Latest email: "Stop building your routine one product at a time" — the same kit story, now hitting their list.
16 Jul
Ads: two whitelisted creator pages joined in, running the kit angle as personal testimony — the classic beauty scale-up move.

Ads, storefront, email and creators — four channels, one message, inside 72 hours. Mia's competitor wasn't running a promo. They were repositioning around average order value before Q4 ad costs kicked in.

The move: get there first

Reading the strategy early is only worth money if you act on it. Mia did three things in the next ten days:

1. Launched her own kit before theirs peaked. Her shade-adapting foundation, primer and brush, packaged at a stronger anchor. The competitor had already proven the demand — she didn't have to test the concept, only ship it.

2. Took the creator lane while it was cheap. Two of her existing customers with mid-size audiences went live with whitelisted ads that week — before the auction filled with kit creative from her competitor's push.

3. Let their urgency sell her offer. When the competitor's countdown timer expired and reset (WatchBack flags those — an evergreen timer, not a real deadline), her ads leaned in: "No fake countdowns. Just the better kit."

None of this required genius. It required seeing the move in week one instead of week five — when the competitor's kit was already everywhere, the creator auction was saturated, and matching them would have looked like copying.
